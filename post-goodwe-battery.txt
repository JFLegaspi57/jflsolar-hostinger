During the GoodWe Global Installer Summit 2026 at GoodWe's headquarters in China, I walked the full battery production line, from loose cells to a packed battery ready for shipping. Every station had a poster explaining what happens there, and I photographed all of them.
A home battery looks simple from the outside: a white box on the wall. Inside, it is dozens of cells, metal connectors, a small computer and a sealed enclosure that all have to work together safely for many years. In this article I go through the production line step by step, in the same order the battery is built, and explain what each step means in plain words.
## A few words you'll see
- Cell: the smallest building block, one rectangular (prismatic) lithium cell. GoodWe's home batteries use lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4), the chemistry we trust for homes because it is thermally stable and lasts thousands of cycles.
- Module: a group of cells stacked and welded together.
- Pack: the finished battery, with one or more modules, the BMS and the enclosure.
- BMS: the Battery Management System, the "brain" that watches every cell and protects the battery.
- OCV: Open Circuit Voltage, the voltage of a cell when nothing is connected to it. It tells you the cell's state of charge and health.
- Internal resistance: how much a cell resists the flow of current. Lower is better; high resistance means heat and lost energy.
- MES: Manufacturing Execution System, the factory software that records what happened to every product at every station.
## Stage 1: Check every cell before anything is built
Step 1, cell loading: the cells arrive on pallets and a robot moves them to the inspection station for incoming quality checks. Nothing goes into a battery before it has been checked.
Step 2, OCV testing of the cells: each cell's QR code is scanned, and its open circuit voltage and internal resistance are measured. The scan starts the cell's traceability record, so its history can be followed through the whole factory.
Why this matters: a battery is only as good as its weakest cell. If one cell has a lower capacity or higher resistance than the others, it will be the first to run empty when discharging and the first to fill up when charging. The BMS then has to limit the whole battery to protect that one cell. Measuring every cell makes sure only cells with matching characteristics are built together.
## Stage 2: Build the module
Step 3, stacking and compression: the cells are stacked in series or parallel and pressed together to the exact module length. In series, the voltages add up; in parallel, the capacities add up. The compression is not just for fitting. LiFePO4 cells expand and contract slightly as they charge and discharge. Holding them under a controlled pressure keeps the inside layers of each cell in good contact, which improves the cell's cycle life.
Step 4, cell welding: the cells are connected with laser welding. According to GoodWe's own poster, weld quality directly affects the battery's safety and how much current it can carry. A good weld has very low resistance; a weak one heats up under load, and heat is the enemy of every battery.
Step 5, OCV testing after welding: the module is tested again for voltage and internal resistance, this time to catch welding defects such as poor contact or a "cold weld", a weld that looks fine but is not properly fused. These defects are hard to see with the eye, but they show up clearly in the measurements.
Step 6, module transfer to the pack line: after manufacturing and inspection, the finished module is lifted and moved to pack assembly, where it becomes part of a complete battery.
## Stage 3: Build the pack
Step 7, module into the enclosure: the module is lifted into the battery case following the electrical connection layout, with the terminals facing the right way. Reversed polarity inside a battery is not a small mistake, so the orientation is checked at this step.
Step 8, pack assembly: the modules, the BMS and the structural parts are brought together into one complete battery system.
Step 9, busbar fastening: the busbars (thick metal connectors between modules and terminals) are bolted down with a tool that monitors the torque and counts the screws. If a screw is missed or not tight enough, the system knows.
This step has a lesson for every installer. A loose terminal is one of the most common causes of hot spots and melted cables in the field. The factory controls torque on every screw, and we should do the same on site: use a torque wrench, follow the manual's values, and recheck connections during maintenance.
Step 10, BMS installation: the BMS is installed to monitor, control and protect the battery. It watches the voltage and temperature of the cells, balances them, and cuts off the battery if something goes outside safe limits. The BMS is also linked to the pack's serial number in the MES, so every battery's electronics and history can be traced.
## Stage 4: Test every battery, not just samples
Step 11, leak testing: the sealed battery is tested with an air leak tester to make sure the enclosure is airtight, so dust and moisture cannot get in. In our hot and humid climate, a good seal is a big part of a long battery life.
Step 12, transfer to the testing line: once assembly is complete, the pack moves to the testing area for full performance checks.
Step 13, EOL testing: EOL stands for End of Line. Every product undergoes a complete electrical performance and safety compliance test to confirm it works as designed and to find any potential safety risk before it leaves the factory.
Step 14, aging testing: each battery system is charged and discharged in simulated cycles. GoodWe's poster says this is done on 100% of the batteries, not on random samples. The test verifies capacity, voltage consistency between cells and other key performance indicators, and only batteries that meet the standard are shipped.
Why this matters: early failures in electronics usually happen in the first hours of use. Running every battery through real charge and discharge cycles in the factory means a weak unit is caught there, not on your wall.
## Stage 5: Packaging
Step 15, packaging: the battery is packed and sealed on an automated line, ready for shipment.
## What this means for you
For homeowners and businesses, this is what you are paying for when you choose a battery from a serious manufacturer: matched cells, controlled welds and torque, a BMS that protects every cell, a sealed enclosure, and a test on every single unit before it ships. Together with full traceability, it also makes warranty support faster, because the factory can look up the history of your specific battery by its serial number.
For installers, the production line is also a reminder of our part of the job. The factory does everything right inside the box. Outside the box, it is up to us: correct polarity, correct torque on every terminal, proper cable sizes, keeping covers and cable glands closed, and setting the battery parameters correctly in the inverter.
